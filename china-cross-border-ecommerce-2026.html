中国跨境电商：规模还在增长，经营逻辑正在改变

平台让交易更容易发生，却没有消除通关、税费、退货和本地交付的成本。中国跨境电商下一阶段的差别，将更多体现在企业如何把一次订单，变成可持续的跨境经营。

产业与供应链中国跨境电商政策与数据截至 2026 年 11 月 11 日
中文English

中国跨境电商的规模仍在扩大，但“增长”不再只由平台流量和低价直邮解释。订单要穿过商品规则、进口税费、仓储配送、退货和售后；在不同国家经营，企业还必须面对不同的产品标准、消费者保护和申报要求。把电商看作一条跨境履约链，才能看清行业正在发生什么。

2.84 万亿元2025 年中国跨境电商进出口总值；海关后续统计口径
+4.8%2025 年同比增幅
1.45 万亿元2026 年上半年进出口总值，同比增长 5.8%

海关数据表明，行业已经形成可观规模。海关总署在 2026 年 1 月发布的初步统计为 2.75 万亿元；之后商务部网站援引海关统计公布的 2025 年总值为 2.84 万亿元、同比增长 4.8%。本文采用后公布的统计数，不把两次发布当作两种可以混加的市场规模。2026 年上半年，跨境电商进出口为 1.45 万亿元，出口 1.16 万亿元、进口 2,918 亿元。统计期、发布时点和修订状态都应一起阅读。

这些总量说明交易活动继续扩张，却不能直接说明某个平台、某个品类或某家企业赚了多少钱。海关统计衡量的是跨境货物贸易金额；行业报告所称的线上零售额、平台 GMV、订单量和商家销售额，覆盖范围与计量方法可能不同。它们不能未经定义核对，就拼成一个“行业总盘子”。

先把三个变化分开看

交易线上化，不代表贸易本身变成轻资产

平台降低了找客户、展示商品和处理订单的门槛。货物一旦跨境，仍需完成分类申报、目的国税费处理、合规标签、配送和售后。销售额增长而库存、退款、物流和获客成本同步上升时，企业的现金回收与利润可能并不同步。平台是交易渠道，不能代替整条经营链。

市场规则正在改变低价包裹的成本结构

美国自 2025 年 5 月 2 日起取消中国及香港商品适用的 800 美元以下小额免税待遇；自 2025 年 8 月 29 日起，美国又将暂停范围扩展至所有国家和地区。不同商品、原产地和运输方式适用的税费仍须按具体规则判断，但低值包裹不再能简单假设为免税入境。

欧盟在 2026 年 7 月 1 日起对价值低于 150 欧元、直送消费者的小包裹实施过渡性 3 欧元关税，按包裹内不同商品类别计收；欧盟理事会另确认自 2026 年 11 月 1 日起实施每件 2 欧元的处理费。关税与处理费是两项不同收费。它们会改变小额订单的到岸成本，但不意味着每一件商品都承担相同的最终费用：申报、品类归类、商品来源和销售安排都会影响实际处理。

这类变化传递的信号很明确：过去依靠单件低价、跨境直邮和简化清关的业务，需要重新核算边际成本。海外仓可能缩短末端配送时间，却会带来备货资金、滞销库存、退货和当地仓配费用。它是经营模式的变化，不是成本自动消失。

履约方式要和订单结构一起理解

海关监管方式代码能帮助说明不同交易路径，但它们不是企业经营能力的排名。9610通常对应跨境电商零售进出口；9710用于跨境电商企业对企业直接出口；9810对应跨境电商出口海外仓。海关在 2024 年优化海外仓监管时，取消了出口海外仓企业的业务模式备案要求；这项调整简化了特定监管手续，并不免除商品申报、税务、目的国进口或产品责任。

直邮适合订单分散、需求尚待验证的商品，但单票处理、国际运输和关税规则会影响成本与体验。海外仓可以支持当地配送和退货，却要求企业预测销量、控制周转并承担库存风险。B2B大货更关注合同、批次、交付和结算安排。三者服务的订单结构不同，不存在对所有企业都更优的单一模式。

真正的本地化也不只是把货放在当地。商品标签和安全要求、进口商责任、退货地址、客服语言、支付与税务处理，共同决定消费者是否能顺利完成一次交易。海外仓只是其中一环。

供应链数字化，解决的是信息衔接

当订单来自多个平台，库存分散在不同仓库，财务又通过 ERP 管理时，重复录入和状态不一致会增加处理成本。系统集成和流程编排可以把订单、发货、仓储状态与财务记录连接起来；它们能否减少错误或缩短处理时间，应以具体部署后的业务数据验证。

技术系统可以减少人工搬运信息，却不能替企业判断商品是否符合当地法规，也不能自动消除库存积压、汇率波动或退货损失。系统价值取决于数据准确性、流程责任划分和异常处理能力。

结论：从一次成交，走向持续经营

跨境电商的增长是真实的，但增长数字本身不代表经营质量。现在更值得观察的是：订单通过什么路径履约，税费和服务成本由谁承担，库存何时转成现金，产品能否持续满足目的地市场要求。

平台流量依然重要。它只是经营的起点。跨境电商下一阶段的竞争，将由商品、合规、交付、售后和数据衔接共同决定。把这些环节逐项说清，才是理解行业规模背后变化的可靠方法。

ENGLISH EDITION

China’s Cross-Border E-Commerce Is Growing. Its Operating Model Is Changing.

Platforms make it easier to find customers and take orders. They do not remove the work of customs clearance, duties, returns or local delivery. The next phase of China’s cross-border e-commerce will depend less on a single traffic channel and more on how businesses turn an online order into a repeatable operating process.

The sector is no longer just a marketplace story. It is a fulfilment, compliance and cash-conversion story.

What the headline figures tell us

China’s customs data put cross-border e-commerce imports and exports at RMB 2.84 trillion in 2025, up 4.8% year on year. First-half 2026 trade reached RMB 1.45 trillion, up 5.8%; exports were RMB 1.16 trillion and imports RMB 291.8 billion. The General Administration of Customs initially reported RMB 2.75 trillion for 2025 in January. A later figure published by China’s Ministry of Commerce and attributed to Customs was RMB 2.84 trillion. This article uses the later figure and keeps the publication timing visible.

These totals show continued expansion, not the profitability of a platform, product category or seller. Customs trade values, platform GMV, retail sales and order counts measure different things. They should not be combined unless their scope and methodology match.

Low-value parcels face a different cost environment

The United States ended the de minimis exemption for goods from China and Hong Kong on 2 May 2025, then extended the suspension to shipments from all countries on 29 August 2025. The applicable treatment still depends on product, origin and shipping details, but sellers can no longer assume that low-value parcels enter duty-free.

In the European Union, a transitional €3 customs duty took effect on 1 July 2026 for eligible consignments valued below €150 and sent directly to consumers. It is charged by category of goods in a parcel. A separate €2 handling fee took effect on 1 November 2026, according to the Council of the EU. The duty and handling fee are distinct measures.

These changes put pressure on low-priced, direct-shipped orders. An overseas warehouse can shorten last-mile delivery and support local returns, but it introduces inventory financing, storage, unsold stock and local fulfilment costs. It changes where costs sit; it does not make them disappear.

Fulfilment models answer different needs

China’s customs codes help describe the route, not rank business models. Code 9610 generally covers cross-border e-commerce retail trade; 9710 covers direct B2B exports; and 9810 covers exports to overseas warehouses. Customs simplified the filing requirement for overseas-warehouse export businesses in 2024. That procedural change does not waive customs declarations, tax obligations, destination-market import rules or product responsibilities.

Direct shipping can fit dispersed orders and products with untested demand, while overseas stock can support faster delivery and local returns. The trade-off is greater exposure to inventory and local operating costs. B2B shipments follow different requirements for contracts, batches, delivery and settlement. No single route fits every order profile.

Digital systems connect information; they do not replace operating discipline

When orders arrive from multiple marketplaces and inventory sits across several warehouses, integration can connect order, shipment, warehouse and finance records. Whether it reduces errors or processing time must be measured in the implementation itself. Accurate source data, clear process ownership and exception handling still matter.

INOSSEM describes ProcessGo Plus as a platform for process modelling, system integration, EDI exchange and warehouse or order coordination. That is the vendor’s product description. This briefing does not independently verify vendor-reported efficiency claims or infer that the results apply across the industry.

The business test is repeatable fulfilment

Cross-border e-commerce is growing, but scale alone does not establish business quality. The more useful questions are how an order is fulfilled, who bears duties and service costs, when inventory turns into cash, and whether the product continues to meet the destination market’s rules.

Traffic remains important. It is only the beginning. The next stage will be shaped by products, compliance, delivery, after-sales service and the quality of information moving between them.
